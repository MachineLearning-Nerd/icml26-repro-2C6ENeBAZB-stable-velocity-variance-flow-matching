   657	\textbf{Ablation on Split Point $\xi$.} The split point $\xi$ defines the boundary between the \emph{low-variance regime} and the \emph{high-variance regime}, and determines the extent of representation alignment. Tab.~\ref{tab:split_point} analyzes the effect of different $\xi$ values at multiple training stages. At early training (100k iterations), a smaller split point ($\xi=0.6$) achieves the best FID, indicating that weaker alignment provides an easier supervisory signal that accelerates initial convergence. As training progresses, $\xi=0.7$ consistently yields the best overall performance, particularly at 400k iterations, where it delivers the best performance. In contrast, a larger split point ($\xi=0.8$) degrades performance, due to noisy supervision introduced from the high-variance regime. Based on these results, we adopt $\xi=0.7$ as the default setting.
